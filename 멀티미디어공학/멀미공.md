## digitizing signal
signal: analog to digital
change in magnitude over a single dimension(time)
extening from 1D time axis to a 2D spatial domain(amplitude)
image represented in 3D(x, y, brightness), if you extract a single horizontal line, you get brightness changes along th x-axis, this look link an audio signal.
many principles of 1D signal processing can be extended to 2D image processing
analog, continuous-time signal, discrete-time signal, digital signal
### analog signal
continuous in both time and amplitude
smoothness: differentiable at every moment
### continuous-time signal
signal is defined at all points in time(continuous)
### discrete-time signal
signal is defined only at specific, distinct moments in time(discrete)
### digital signal
discrete in both time and amplitude
defined only at specific points in time, its amplitude can only be one of a finite, predetermined set of values
from nature to computing, Analog-to-Digital conversion(ADC) or Digitization
### how ADC
step1 sampling: in time, the sampling frequency is at least twice the highest frequency in the signal(Nyquist Theorem)
step2 quantization: in amplitude, mapping to the closest value from level(bit depth)
## Frequency Domain
simple periodic wave can be descrived by a sinusoidal function but complex wave cannot
not only time domain but also frequency components
input image = low frequency signal + high frequency signal(moving, edge)
### Fourier Theorem
any periodic waveform can be expressed as the sum of simple sinusoidal waves with different amplitudes, frequencies, and phases
Euler's Formula combines amplitude and phase into a single complex number, essential for DSP(digital signal processor)
Fourier Series: decompose a periodic funtion
this method requires periodic signal so is difficult to apply to most real world
Fourier Transform: make period approach infinity, signal becomes non-periodic(time to frequency)
Fourier Series: to Periodic signal, discretee spectrum, smmation
Fourier Transform: to non-periodic signal, continuous spectrum, integral
## theory into practice
ADC process(audio EQ)
input: analog signal
ADC(digitization): sampling + quantization -> digital
analysis: fourier transform
processing: manipulate the frequency spectrum
synthesis & output: inverse fourier transform(frequency to time)

orthogonal expansion & weighted sum
original signal exists in time domain

## sampling
converting continuous-time signal into a discrete-time signal, reading the amplitude at regular time intervals.
Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem: the sampling frequency f_s must be greater than twice the maximum frequency f_max
aliasing: when you sample lower than Nyquist Rate, the high-frequency components reconstructed as lower frequency, f_alias = f_s - f-original
## quantization
after sampling, time is discrete but still amplitude is continuous
mapping to a finite, limited number of discrete levels with bits
noise: difference between real signal and quantized representation
SQNR: signal to quantization noise ratio, higher SQNR less error, +1 bit +6 dB
linear quantization: same interval, midrise(up down from zero) and midtread(zero is a level)
non-linear quantization: companding(compressing and expanding), small steps for low-amplitude and large steps for high-amplitude, human perception is more sensitive to changes in quiet sounds.

## sound
sound is a wave, the transmission of vibrations through medium(air)
transverse wave(횡파): displacement of medium is perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation
longitudinal wave(종파): displacement of medium is parallel to the direction of wave propagation
### fundamental properties of waves
reflection(반사): a wave bounces back when it hits a surface
diffraction(회절): a wave bends around obstacles or spread through small openings.
refaction(굴절): a wave changes direction whien it passes into a medium with a different speed
## auditory system

middle ear: amplifying vibrations, sound waves strike the eardrum, amplifying
inner ear: converting into neural signal, cochlea convert vibrations into neural signals

## masking effect
signal sound is obscure by masker sound
### temporal masking
blinded by loudness in time
our auditory system requires recovery time after/before loud stimulus, louder masking longer recovery time
forward masking: masker first signal second, gap is up to 200ms
backward masking: signal first masker second, gap is less than 20ms
### frequency masking
drowned out by nearby frequencies
right-hand tail effect: masker around 4kHz is strongest and extends toward higer frequencies
higher masker frequency broader masking coverage
critical band: human auditory system processes frequencies in distinct, width of critical bands changes with frequency(narrow at low, wide at figh)
### combined masking effect
single masker act across both time axis and frequency axis
loud sound obscure close time and close frequency
compression: discard inaudible range
## advanced perceptual cues & laws
out brain use IID(interaural intensity diffefence) and ITD(interaural time difference) to determine direction of sound source
IID: louder sound closer source(one source but two ear)
ITD: faster sound closer source(one source but two ear)
cocktail party effect: human can focus single auditory simulus with many others
precedence effect(haas effect): when over 2 similar sources, human perceives direction of first arriving sound
### weber's law
stronger original stimulus larger change needed for notice, implication for non-linear quantization
delta responce = delta stimulus / stimulus
more bits finer resolution to quiet sounds(fewer bits coarser resolution to loud sounds)
